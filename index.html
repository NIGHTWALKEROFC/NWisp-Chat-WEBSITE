The idea

Three things NWisp is built around

Nobody in the middle

Every message is end-to-end encrypted on the Signal Protocol — the same X3DH + Double Ratchet design used by other privacy-first messengers, so keys change constantly and old messages stay unreadable even if a later key leaks.

Nothing sits around forever

Messages are relayed and then removed server-side, capped at 24 hours whether they were delivered or not. Stories disappear after a day. There's no permanent server-side chat history to lose control of.

You control who sees you

A username-based public identity, contact requests instead of open discovery, blocking that's enforced at the delivery layer, and hidden chats for conversations you'd rather keep out of your main list.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is NWisp actually end-to-end encrypted, or is that marketing?

It's real. Every chat runs the Signal Protocol — X3DH for the initial key exchange, then the Double Ratchet for every message after that — meaning encryption keys keep changing and the server relaying messages never has access to plaintext.

Does NWisp have group chats now?

Yes. You can create a group, add contacts directly or invite people who need to accept, set admins, and restrict sending to admins only if you want. Every group message is still end-to-end encrypted to each member.

Can I make voice or video calls?

Not yet — that's still on the roadmap. You can record and send voice messages in any chat right now, but live calls aren't built.

How do I know I'm really talking to the right person?

Each conversation has a safety number you and the other person can compare through a separate channel (a call, in person, etc.). If it matches on both ends, no one is intercepting the conversation.

What happens to my messages after 24 hours?

They're removed from the relay server, whether they were delivered or not. There's no permanent server-side chat history. Anything you want to keep needs to stay on your device.

Do I need a phone number to sign up?

No. Sign-up only needs a username, an email address (kept private), and a password. Your public identity to other people is just your username.

What if someone gets access to my phone?

App lock, hidden chats with their own code and optional PIN, and the fact that your account can only be signed in on one device at a time all help here — plus screenshot and screen-recording protection inside chats, which is on by default.

Is it free?

Yes, completely. No premium tier, no ads, no paid unlocks. That may change if hosting costs become unsustainable, but there's no plan for that right now.

Is the code open source?

Not right now. It may open up later, but the current priority is finishing the app itself.

Who's building this?

One person, as an independent project — see the Contact section below for how to reach out.
